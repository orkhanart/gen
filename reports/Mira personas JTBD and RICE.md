# Mira's personas converge on private group catch-up

Mira (bot **@mira**, site mira.tg, built by The Open Platform; "Miro" in the user's notes) serves **eight distinct personas**, but **four carry a design proposal**. They are the **Catch-up Member** muted in busy groups, the **Community Keeper** who decides whether Mira stays in a group, the **Telegram-native Team Operator** who needs chat turned into tracked work, and the **CIS Workaround Professional**, the likeliest person to pay in Stars. The job with the strongest evidence across them is getting a **private, topic-separated recap of a busy group**. That job is also the route by which **more than a third of Mira's new users arrive** ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/05/20/telegrams-ai-agent-ecosystem-mira/)). Twenty-five feature ideas were scored with RICE against Mira's company-reported base: about 1M monthly users, 150K–175K groups and a 0.4% tool-connection rate. Two tie for first at **240,000**: a private "catch me up" recap and starter capability chips. Next, at **200,000** each, come a group-to-DM "do this for me" handoff and a single price meter with capped spending. A streamed "agent is working" layer follows at **160,000**. That ranking supports leading the proposal with one journey: the group recap, then a private nudge, then a DM with the task pre-loaded, then the first connected tool, all wrapped in a consent ladder, with the meter as the second chapter. This confirms the hero story from the earlier research. It moves the speed layer up and moves money delegation and a standalone memory screen down. One design tension runs through everything: **a recap needs Mira to read the group, while trust needs Mira to see as little as possible**. Each step up in capability must therefore be a visible step that an admin grants. All inputs rest on search snippets, GitHub sources and Mira's unaudited figures. The RICE values are informed estimates, and none is rated at 100% confidence.

## Evidence rests on snippets, GitHub and Mira's own numbers

The research behind this report could open very few primary web pages. The session's network policy blocked Reddit, telegram.org, X, Habr, vc.ru and most press sites, and the Firecrawl scraper had no credits. The sources read directly were on GitHub: Mira's docs repo ([Mira-Agent/mira-wiki](https://github.com/Mira-Agent/mira-wiki)), a hackathon developer's real @mira chat export ([mira-harness](https://github.com/Masashi-Ono0611/mira-harness)), issue threads on anti-spam, summarizer and Telegram Desktop repos, and READMEs of do-it-yourself bots. Telegram's API documentation was read through mirrors. **Everything else comes from search-result titles, snippets and summaries.** The underlying notes label it as snippet, paraphrase or vendor material, and many vendor sources sell competing tools. **No Mira user was interviewed.** Every persona is a composite built from public evidence, such as tools people built, complaints, surveys and product docs. Every job-to-be-done is inferred and should be checked with in-bot surveys or interviews.

Mira's own figures are **company-reported, unaudited and inconsistent across its pages**. They are about 1M monthly users ([Mira blog](https://mira.tg/blog/mira-1-million-monthly-users)), 150K+ groups ([Team Agent](https://mira.tg/products/team-agent)) or 175K ([H1 report](https://mira.tg/report)), about 40% of users who "put Mira to work on a real task", and 0.4% who connect an external service. Mira publishes **no split by country, language, device or group type**, so persona sizes cannot be measured. The RICE inputs are therefore estimates. Each Reach figure comes from a stated assumption, and Confidence follows a fixed rule. **80%** goes to estimates backed by Mira-specific evidence or strong evidence from several independent sources. **50%** goes to estimates that rest on thin or inferred evidence. **No item gets 100%**, because Mira has published no data on how any of the proposed fixes would perform.

## Eleven candidate personas collapse into eight, four of them primary

The four segment studies (everyday consumers, community admins and members, crypto/TON users, and work teams and small businesses) proposed eleven candidate personas and three sub-variants. The table shows how they were consolidated. Weak or overlapping candidates were merged into the persona whose jobs they share. A persona is **primary** if it has strong evidence and is tied directly to a number Mira reports: its group count, its tool-connection rate or its Pro revenue. A persona is **secondary** if its Mira-specific evidence is thin, its reachable pool is small, or its main job depends on a platform feature with tight limits.

### Primary 1: the Catch-up Member is the returner who brings Mira in

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

No Telegram-specific survey of members exists |
| Goals | Rejoin a conversation in seconds. Learn what was decided and what needs them. Stay muted without missing what matters |
| Current tools and workarounds | Mute, archive, folders, scrolling, asking a friend. A technical minority self-hosts summarizer bots: one has 205 stars and asks "Feel scared when seeing 2000+ unread in groups?" ([telegram-summary-bot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/asukaminato0721/telegram-summary-bot/main/README.md)). Telegram's native AI summaries cover long channel posts and Instant View, not group chats ([Telegram blog](https://telegram.org/blog/new-design-ai-summaries)) |
| Top pains (ranked) | 1. Backlogs of "thousands (or tens of thousands) of unread messages" ([telegram-mcp #162](https://github.com/chigwell/telegram-mcp/issues/162)). 2. | Unmet needs | A zero-setup, private recap separated by topic, with "needs you" items and jump links. Plain-language search of history. Per-member disclosure and opt-out |
| Trust and privacy | Welcomes AI they summon. Likely prefers private delivery, which is an inference to test |
| Willingness to pay | Low on their own. The recap should stay free, because its value is converting the member into a personal user |
| Discovery and use of Mira | Sees Mira at work in a shared chat. Praise: Mira "summarized 200 group chat messages while I was away in 10 seconds… no new app, no new tab" ([X @defileo](https://x.com/defileo/status/2073053615988830333)) |
| Evidence strength | **High** for the catch-up job: verbatim GitHub issues, DIY tools, Mira praise and the company's discovery figure. **Medium** for the Bystander. Whether members prefer private or public delivery is untested |

### Primary 2: the Community Keeper is the gatekeeper who can remove Mira

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Archetype and segment | "The bouncer." A volunteer or part-paid admin or moderator of crypto/Web3, tech, hobby and local groups. Absorbs the Web3 community admin and the channel owner who runs a discussion group |
| Context and markets | Often runs several groups. The most-reacted request on the tg-spam bot is one instance for many chats ([tg-spam #100](https://github.com/umputun/tg-spam/issues/100)). Round-the-clock moderation is a paid job in crypto ([CryptoJobsList](https://cryptojobslist.com/talent/telegram-moderator)). Mira's 150K–175K groups are roughly **7–9% of the 2M monthly active groups** that Rose claims ([Rose FAQ](https://missrose.org/FAQ/)) |
| Goals | Keep spam and scammers out. Keep newcomers oriented and the group alive. Never be embarrassed by a bot |
| Current tools and workarounds | Rose, Combot and its shared ban list, Shieldy, GroupHelp, captcha bots, and self-hosted tg-spam, which is adding LLM checks. Telegram's own anti-spam is a single on/off switch that a guide says "often triggers incorrectly" ([tginfo](https://tginfo.me/?p=7964)). Since June 2026 there are also Telegram's AI Guardians for screening joins ([ElevenForum copy of Telegram blog](https://www.elevenforum.com/t/telegram-june-update-smartwatch-apps-rich-text-for-bots-ai-guardians-for-groups-and-much-more.47480/)) |
| Top pains (ranked) | 1. Spam formats that slip past rule-based bots, such as fake forwards the /spam command cannot remove ([tg-spam #299](https://github.com/umputun/tg-spam/issues/299)) and "Thank you" quote-replies ([#376](https://github.com/umputun/tg-spam/issues/376)). 2. Fake "Safeguard" verification bots, up about **2,000%** in winter 2024–25 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/malicious-telegram-group-scams-have-increased-2000-since-november-scam-sniffer)). 3. Removals that can't be audited ([#181](https://github.com/umputun/tg-spam/issues/181)). 4. The same newcomer questions every day; a vendor estimates 50–75% are "standard" ([Mava](https://mava.app/blog/customer-support-in-web3-is-broken)). 5. Bot clutter in the chat ([#175](https://github.com/umputun/tg-spam/issues/175)). 6. Configuring each group separately |
| Unmet needs | A judgment layer that sits beside Rose, not in place of it. Removals that explain themselves, with one-tap restore. FAQ answers drawn from pinned posts. One policy across many groups. Full control over when and how the agent speaks |
| Trust and privacy | Wants role tiers for who may command an agent ([hermes-agent #527](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/issues/527), +26) and mention-only behaviour. Rejects bots that self-promote, act unpredictably, ask members to "verify", or want admin rights without a reason. Mira's add link asks for admin rights up front ([Mira wiki](https://wiki.mira.tg/group-chats/overview)) |
| Willingness to pay | Unknown. Volunteers are near zero. Projects already pay for moderators and support tools, so a group tier paid by the project is plausible but has no evidence |
| Discovery and use of Mira | Adds Mira through the group-add deep link. Moderation needs admin rights to delete, warn and mute ([Mira wiki](https://wiki.mira.tg/group-chats/moderation)). The channel-owner variant wants drafts approved before publishing ([Telega](https://telega.io/blog/autoposter-bot)); it is parked for lack of first-person evidence |
| Evidence strength | **Medium-High.** Verbatim GitHub issues cover spam and control. The FAQ workload rests on vendor sources. No first-person Reddit posts were found |

**Six problems reach six or more personas**: unread overload (P1), scams (P3), privacy (P5), setup friction (P7), opaque pricing (P8) and slow flows (P11). Fixing them pays off across the whole user base, which is why the RICE leaders below come from these rows. Overload is not just an inconvenience. For the Team Operator it is how commitments get lost. For the Speed Trader it is a timing and adverse-selection problem: the risk is ending up as "exit liquidity", buying as insiders sell. For the Catch-up Member it is the reason they first call Mira. Scams form the most dangerous shared row because they decide how anyone receives *any* bot. Fake "Safeguard" bots have taught crypto groups to distrust bots that ask them to verify. The CIS Professional arrives assuming most bots are cheap wrappers. Mira pays that trust tax before it says a word.

The privacy row is universal, but it **means something different for nearly every persona**. The CIS Professional fears access by the state. The Student-Creator fears misuse of their face. The Team Operator fears exposing linked work accounts. The Service Business Owner worries that customers' messages are read unencrypted by an AI provider. The Bystander fears being remembered by a bot they never chose. A single "Private Mode" toggle answers none of these fears specifically. That is the strongest argument for scoped, visible controls: per-group read rungs, per-fact memory receipts, a face-upload promise and a per-connector access log, each placed where its fear arises. Pricing works the same way. The CIS Professional's complaint is opacity: they would pay, but felt deceived. The Student-Creator and Farmer face a price level 4–6× above the roughly $4.50 regional AI plans ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/22/after-india-openai-launches-its-affordable-chatgpt-go-plan-in-indonesia/)). So a clear meter and regional pricing are two separate fixes.

The jobs fall into three bundles. The first is **pull-based triage** (CM-1, CM-2, TO-1, ST-1): the user summons Mira to digest a busy context. The evidence here is strongest because people have built tools to do it themselves. The second is **delegation through me, with a preview** (KP-2, TO-3, ST-2, SB-1 to SB-3). The same specification shows up independently in anti-spam issues, DIY secretary bots, crypto surveys and the Walt agent-account design: act only when called, inside limits I set, through my own account, with commitments routed to me for approval. That convergence across unrelated communities is the most important pattern in the data. These trust jobs are conditions users will never ask for, yet the delegation jobs fail without them. Three platform constraints shape the list more than any user evidence does. First, **Guest Mode gives a bot one reply and no message history** ([guest bots](https://core.telegram.org/bots/features#guest-bots)). In privacy mode a member bot sees only mentions, replies and commands. A self-hosted summarizer also notes it can see only messages sent "after the bot join the group" ([telegram-summary-bot](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/asukaminato0721/telegram-summary-bot/main/README.md)). So the top-rated feature, F01, works only on the admin rung of F05's consent ladder. The ladder is therefore what unlocks the recap, not a compliance add-on. That rung costs trust in public, because "users can always see a bot's current privacy setting in the list of group members" ([privacy mode](https://core.telegram.org/bots/features#privacy-mode)). Second, **streaming drafts work only in private chats**, and groups are capped at 20 bot messages a minute. Group features therefore have to use one edited status message, plus ephemeral messages for anything personal. Third, **only one business bot can be connected per account** ([Secretary bots](https://core.telegram.org/bots/features#secretary-bots)). The service-business feature (F12) is an all-or-nothing contest for that single slot, which is why its onboarding matters so much.

## RICE puts the group-to-DM journey and the meter on top

### Reach starts from Mira's reported base and stated assumptions

Every Reach figure is **users per quarter**, built from the base figures below. Group-based features are converted to users so that all scores share one unit. Reach counts the people who interact with or make a decision through a feature, not passive viewers. **Impact** measures how far each reached user moves toward trusted, connected or paying use: 3 = massive, 2 = high, 1 = medium, 0.5 = low, 0.25 = minimal. **Confidence** follows the 80%/50% rule above. **Effort** is in person-months of combined design, engineering and QA. It assumes Mira reuses its existing summaries, memory, Composio connectors, reminders and Stars billing. **Score = (R × I × C) / E.**

**The private recap (F01) leads** because it combines the widest high-impact reach with the strongest evidence in the set. Members have built more than a dozen DIY summarizers to do this job. The most-starred one draws explicit complaints about mixed topics. Mira's best organic praise is about exactly this use. The redesign is cheap because Mira already summarizes groups: the change is delivering the recap privately, separating it by topic and pulling out "needs you" items. **Starter chips (F03) tie for first on cost alone.** They are a one-person-month fix for a "What can you actually do?" problem that the company's own funnel supports, though the impact on each user is only medium. **The handoff (F02) and the meter (F13) follow at 200,000** for different reasons. F02 is the step that turns Mira's main discovery channel into personal use. F13 removes the churn trigger with the most direct Mira-specific evidence: a paying user who called Pro deception, and four different Pro prices across Mira's own pages. **The streaming layer (F17) ranks fifth on sheer reach.** Almost every DM user waits, and Telegram has made Thinking, streaming and Stop native, so this is the one item whose rank depends most on what Mira has already shipped.

The low ranks of the money and business features are just as informative. F16, F12 and F23 carry Impact 2–3 but reach only 10,000–20,000 users today. Mira's wallet is on testnet, Walt's agent trading is weeks old, and the business-bot slot is exclusive. **RICE measures the current user base, not strategic fit.** It therefore underrates exactly the features TOP's ecosystem (Walt, TON Agentic Wallets, the planned spending sub-wallet) is built to pursue. The proposal should present them as a deliberate later phase, not a mistake in the scoring.

The RICE results largely confirm the earlier research's recommended shape and sharpen it in three places. The hero journey should run: **a member asks for a private recap (F01) → taps "Get my own" (F02) → lands in a DM with the task pre-loaded and starter chips (F03) → connects one tool at the moment it helps (F04)**. The consent ladder (F05) sits underneath as the mechanism that grants the recap its read access, and memory receipts appear at each step. That journey covers four of the top six scores. It also attacks the 0.4% connection rate the company calls its bottleneck, through the growth loop that brings in a third of new users. **The meter (F13 with F14) is the second chapter.** It is the most robust high scorer, it holds the strongest Mira-specific complaint, and it belongs to the monetization area the sibling PM role owns. The first sharpening is that the **streaming layer (F17) deserves its own chapter** rather than a footnote, if a device check shows Mira still does not stream. The second is that **pull should come before push**: the reactive recap outranks the proactive digest (F19), which is what users praise and avoids the notification-fatigue risk. The third is that **money delegation (F16) is an appendix** showing the trust pattern (preview, scoped approval, kill switch) applied to Walt. It demonstrates fluency with TOP's ecosystem without claiming a reach it lacks today.

To keep the scoring honest, the proposal should present the table as a **hypothesis ledger to re-score with Mira's telemetry**, not as a verdict. Every Reach figure would change with measured data: active members per group, recap invocations, the share of users meeting a paid action, the country mix and the Mira Daily subscriber count. The proposal should also commit to instrumenting a set of metrics: recap-to-DM conversion, tool-connection rate against the 0.4% baseline, time to first useful reply, confirmation-card expiry rate, paid conversion alongside refund and complaint rates, and the rate at which groups remove Mira after seeing the consent card.

## Conclusion

The persona work changes how Mira's opportunity should be understood. The earlier research treated activation, consent and pricing as three problems. Across eight personas, they turn out to be **one problem: users don't trust Mira enough to let it do more**. A group member will not let Mira read the group, a user will not connect Gmail, and a payer will not renew unless each can see what Mira sees, what it will do and what it costs. The recap is the rare feature that earns that trust and delivers value in the same moment, because the user summons it, it is private and it is visibly useful. That is why it beats features with larger theoretical impact. For a designer the implication is concrete. In Telegram, each rung of Mira's visibility is already visible to everyone in the member list, so **the consent card is the onboarding**, and the first recap is its payoff.

The consolidation also exposes a gap between who pays and who arrives. Mira's likeliest payers are CIS professionals buying Stars through SBP, while its biggest arrival channels are mass-market group members and Indian students anchored at ₹0. A single 1,500-Star Pro tier cannot serve both, so pricing design is persona design. Finally, the clearest finding comes from people with no connection to each other: anti-spam maintainers, DIY secretary-bot builders, crypto survey respondents and Walt's own product team all specify the same thing. They want an agent that acts only when called, within limits the user sets, through the user's own account, and that sends commitments back for approval. Mira can treat that specification as validated even without interviews, and build every feature in the list on it.
